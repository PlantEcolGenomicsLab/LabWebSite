## The idea

Different environments select for different traits, and over time this leaves a mark on the
genomes of the species living there. If species are best adapted to their native habitats, then
across many species we expect habitat, adaptive traits and the genes that shape them to vary
together, even among distantly related species. Mapping these links from **genotype to phenotype
to fitness** is the key to understanding, and eventually designing, climate resilience.

## Our approach

- **Habitats as phenotypes.** Our pipeline combines species occurrence records with climate and
  soil databases to characterize the habitat of any species, refined by the new data from our
  [species sampling](../species/).
- **Genes behind habitats.** Phylogenetic mixed models link these habitat profiles to gene-level
  changes predicted by our [AI models](../ai-genomics/), highlighting genes and processes that
  have repeatedly been under selection.
- **Common gardens.** Controlled heat and salt stress experiments on about 100 representative
  grass species measure molecular and whole-plant traits directly, filling in the missing
  phenotype layer between genes and environments.

## Traits we focus on

- **Heat and salt resilience**: the most pressing challenges for agriculture and grasslands
  in Taiwan and the region.
- **Nutrient use**: building on our work on rhizosphere nitrogen cycling across grasses.
- **Life history**: the evolution of perennial and annual growth, key to sustainable nutrient
  cycling.
- **Cold tolerance**: including freezing tolerance in wild relatives of crops.

The resulting candidate genes and variants are concrete targets for breeding climate-resilient
crops and for guiding conservation.
